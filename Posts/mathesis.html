The Specter of Mathesis: Structure, Substance, and the Quarrel over the Foundations of Mathematics

Published: 2026-11-25 · Full treatise: PDF, 115 pp.

0. Two Problems under One Name

Mathesis universalis has always been a name for two problems that refuse to stay apart: the first internal, whether the heterogeneous branches of mathematics can be built and articulated from a common ground without losing their structural, operational and inferential organisation; the second external and far more ambitious, whether mathematics, so unified, may legislate for the rest of knowledge. Descartes and Leibniz fused the two, Kant drove a wedge between them, and the twentieth century, having inherited the wedge, mostly stopped asking the first question, as if ZFC had answered it by default. It has not. The contemporary quarrel over foundations—the cumulative hierarchy on one side, potential structuralism proved by categories and homotopy types on the other—is precisely the arena in which the first problem is now being decided, and the terrain is one the classical authors could not have foreseen: the question is no longer what things are made of but what it means for two of them to be the same. My wager is that any configuration of foundations capable of carrying the unificatory role of mathesis must have Homotopy Type Theory at its core, on the ground that it is the first framework in which identity, equivalence and their higher coherences are internal to the theory rather than administered from outside it—a ground which implies neither its truth nor its finality. Two disclaimers bound the wager, since ZFC does not, of itself, commit anyone to substantialism, and categories or types do not, by sheer formal architecture, establish a relational ontology; what I compare is what each framework makes expressible, and the ontology read off that comparison is interpretation, which I shall flag whenever it threatens to pass for a theorem.

I. Substance: The Hierarchy and Its Two Failures

The iterative conception tells a story of formation, \[ V_0=\varnothing,\qquad V_{\alpha+1}=\mathcal{P}(V_\alpha),\qquad V_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}V_\alpha,\qquad V=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}}V_\alpha, \] in which elements come first and collections after, the relata preceding the relation that gathers them; but the story only becomes an ontology once one sees how it fixes identity, and it does so through two axioms working in tandem. Extensionality, \[ \forall x\,\forall y\,\big[\forall z\,(z\in x\leftrightarrow z\in y)\rightarrow x=y\big], \] declares that a set is nothing over and above its members; Foundation, \(\forall x\,[\,x\neq\varnothing\rightarrow\exists y\in x\,(y\cap x=\varnothing)\,]\), makes \(\in\) well-founded, licenses \(\in\)-induction and the rank \(\rho(x)=\sup\{\rho(y)+1: y\in x\}\), and with it the equivalences \(x\in V_\alpha\Leftrightarrow\rho(x)<\alpha\) and \(\text{Foundation}\Leftrightarrow V=\mathrm{WF}\). Taken together, they make the identity of every set the identity of its hereditary membership tree, decided by recursion all the way down to \(\varnothing\). This is what Awodey and Shulman call material identity, and it is the exact sense in which I speak of substance: a self-identical bearer whose identity is settled before, and independently of, any relation it may enter. A consequence follows which is a theorem rather than a metaphor. If \(\pi:(T,\in)\to(T,\in)\) is an automorphism of a transitive set, then \(\pi(x)=\{\pi(y):y\in x\}\), and \(\in\)-induction yields \(\pi=\mathrm{id}_T\): the material universe is rigid. Nothing in \(V\) is symmetric with anything but itself; every set is, so to speak, its own complete concept. (In pure ZFC the ‘objects’ antecedent to sets are themselves earlier sets; object-priority acquires its literal form only in ZFU, where \(V_0(A)=A\) is a stock of urelements—which locates the substantialist reading rather than refuting it, since ZFU exhibits in full the model that ZFC carries only in outline.) [PDF, Ch. 1 §II]

Rigid in each of its objects, the hierarchy is nonetheless indeterminate as a totality, and the contrast is instructive. It is indeterminate in height benignly: there is no last stage, Russell’s class under Foundation coincides with the proper class \(V\), and the paradox reduces to the statement that the universe is not a set. It is indeterminate in width in a way that no foundational optimism can absorb. Since \(|V_{\omega+\alpha}|=\beth_\alpha\), the Continuum Hypothesis \(2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1\) asks precisely how thick the hierarchy is at stage \(\omega+1\), whether \(\mathcal{P}(V_\omega)\) is as small as it could be; and Gödel’s \(L\) and Cohen’s forcing give \(\mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})\Rightarrow\mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC}+\mathrm{CH})\) and \(\mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})\Rightarrow\mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC}+\neg\mathrm{CH})\). The iterative picture does not even earn all of its own axioms: it delivers Extensionality, Separation, Pairing, Union, Power Set and Foundation almost by inspection, but not Infinity (\(V_\omega\) satisfies everything else) nor Replacement (\(V_{\omega+\omega}\models\mathrm{Z}\), while \(n\mapsto V_{\omega+n}\) has cofinal range). Those two are held on fecundity and history, and ZFC’s standing owes more to a century of success, formal adaptability and institutional inertia than to any privileged epistemic status—‘everything works; why change?’ is a respectable argument, though not a foundational one [PDF, Ch. 1 §I.2]. So the substantialist universe offers maximal individuation of each object together with indeterminacy of the whole: every subset of \(\omega\) that exists is fully determined by its members, and the axioms cannot say how many exist. That is already a strange profile for an ontology of substances. But the two failures that matter for unity lie elsewhere, and they are both consequences of rigidity.

The first is the opacity of encoding. Every mathematical object is, or is encoded by, a set—\((a,b):=\{\{a\},\{a,b\}\}\), functions as sets of pairs, \(\mathbb{N}:=\omega\), \(\mathbb{Z}:=(\omega\times\omega)/\!\sim\) with \((a,b)\sim(c,d)\Leftrightarrow a+d=b+c\), \(\mathbb{R}\) as Dedekind cuts or as classes of Cauchy sequences, a group as a tuple \((G,m,e,\iota)\in V\)—and each encoding is a choice which the theory then treats as a fact. Since identity is material, the language sees through the encoding to its membership tree and answers questions about the implementation as though they were questions about the object. Hence the junk theorems: \(\{a\}\in(a,b)\); \(0\in 1\) and \(1\in 3\) under von Neumann; \(q\in\sqrt{2}\) for every rational \(q<\sqrt 2\) if reals are cuts, false or ill-typed if they are equivalence classes; \(\pi\cap e\) a perfectly good set. Benacerraf’s parable is this phenomenon in its purest form. Take \(S_N(x)=x\cup\{x\}\) and \(S_Z(x)=\{x\}\), both from \(\varnothing\): both progressions satisfy Peano, they agree on every arithmetical truth, and yet \(1_N\in 3_N\) while \(1_Z\notin 3_Z\), \(3_N\in 17_N\) while the only element of \(17_Z\) is \(16_Z\). Neither Ernie nor Johnny can produce an arithmetical reason to prefer his encoding, because every such reason applies to the other, and Benacerraf concludes that no set is the number 3. I draw a sharper conclusion: the junk is a structural consequence of material identity itself, and no cleverer choice of encoding will cure it. A rigid universe cannot host an object that is only a position; whatever occupies the position brings its membership tree along, and the language, which cannot be prevented from looking, will find something there. Working mathematicians never assert these theorems, but nothing in the theory stops them: their exclusion is a matter of discipline, not of grammar.

The second failure is deeper, and it concerns invariance. For structures \(\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}\) of a signature \(\Sigma\) and an isomorphism \(h:\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}\), the transfer lemma gives, for every \(\Sigma\)-formula \(\varphi\), \[ \mathfrak{A}\models\varphi[\bar a]\iff\mathfrak{B}\models\varphi[h(\bar a)], \] and this is the indiscernibility of isomorphs on which all structural mathematics silently relies. But it holds only for the object language \(\Sigma\), never for the ambient \(\mathcal{L}_\in\). Let \(\psi(\mathfrak{N})\) be the \(\mathcal{L}_\in\)-formula \(S(0)\in S(S(S(0)))\): ZFC proves \(\mathfrak{N}_N\cong\mathfrak{N}_Z\), proves \(\psi(\mathfrak{N}_N)\), and proves \(\neg\psi(\mathfrak{N}_Z)\). The general principle \[ \mathfrak{A}\cong\mathfrak{B}\;\Longrightarrow\;\big(\Phi(\mathfrak{A})\leftrightarrow\Phi(\mathfrak{B})\big)\qquad(\Phi\in\mathrm{Form}(\mathcal{L}_\in)) \] is therefore refutable in ZFC, which is a stronger verdict than independence would be. Structural invariance survives only as a metatheorem about a fragment of the language, and the set theorist who wants to honour it must quotient—but the isomorphism class of \(\mathfrak{N}\) is a proper class, and the only way to name it is Scott’s trick, \([\mathfrak{A}]:=\{\mathfrak{B}\cong\mathfrak{A}:\rho(\mathfrak{B})\ \text{minimal}\}\), which buys invariance by reinvoking rank, the very material apparatus that created the problem. There remains the question of what kind of thing identity is here. \(x=y\) is a bare proposition, decided by Extensionality; an isomorphism \(h\) is a set of witnesses, possibly many, and distinct witnesses are not interchangeable. ZFC can state both, but it has no means of relating them: identity does not remember how two things are the same, and so the question that univalence will later pose—whether sameness and identity can be identified—cannot even be formulated. To this I add the objection I regard as decisive, which comes from Cavaillès. If mathematics develops by internal necessity, each stage engendering the next by thematising its own operations, unity is genetic; and the representability of every structure in \(V\) explains nothing of that movement. Large cardinals, inner models and forcing are among the deepest things mathematics has produced, but they act on \(V\) from the outside, driven by independence and by the decision to adopt new axioms. ZFC gives mathematics a common substrate. It does not give it a history.

II. Arrows: Individuation up to Isomorphism

Philosophers of ‘structure’ have, with remarkable consistency, ignored the discipline that actually defines it. Their structures are Bourbaki tuples \(\mathfrak{B}=\langle A;\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C};\Phi\rangle\), syntax-dependent by construction, so that a topology on \(X\) given by its opens \(\mathcal{O}(X)\), by its closed sets \(\mathcal{C}(X)\), or by a Kuratowski operator \(\mathrm{cl}\) yields three distinct structures, identified only by a translation belonging to none of them. Category theory removes the choice, and the mechanism by which it does so is the universal property. A product of \(a\) and \(b\) is not a set of pairs but any \(p\) with \(\pi_1:p\to a\), \(\pi_2:p\to b\) such that for every \(x\) the map \[ \mathscr{C}(x,p)\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\mathscr{C}(x,a)\times\mathscr{C}(x,b),\qquad f\mapsto(\pi_1 f,\pi_2 f), \] is a bijection natural in \(x\); more generally, a limit of \(D:\mathscr{J}\to\mathscr{C}\) is a representing object for cones, \(\mathrm{Cone}(x,D)\cong\mathscr{C}(x,\lim D)\). What a universal property specifies is a role, not an occupant: anything that plays it is admissible, and any two occupants agree through a unique isomorphism compatible with the structure maps. ‘The’ product is thus an abuse of the definite article, licensed by uniqueness up to unique isomorphism and, when one insists on chosen limits for a large category, paid for with a global choice principle. The natural numbers object is the example that matters here: \(1\xrightarrow{0}N\xrightarrow{s}N\) is initial among such diagrams, i.e. for every \(1\xrightarrow{x}X\xrightarrow{t}X\) there is a unique \(h\) with \(h\circ 0=x\) and \(h\circ s=t\circ h\),

and both \((\omega,\varnothing,S_N)\) and \((\omega_Z,\varnothing,S_Z)\) are natural numbers objects in \(\mathbf{Set}\), hence related by a unique structure-preserving isomorphism. Benacerraf’s junk does not survive the translation, and the reason is grammatical, not disciplinary: the language of arrows has no \(\in\). In ETCS an element of \(X\) is a global element \(1\to X\), and membership is always relative—\(x\in_X m\) means that \(x\) factors through a mono \(m:A\rightarrowtail X\)—so ‘\(1\in 3\)’, which would ask a global element of \(N\) to be a member of another global element of \(N\), is not false but ill-formed. Adjunctions generalise the move. A functor \(G:\mathscr{D}\to\mathscr{C}\) has a left adjoint exactly when every \(c\) admits a universal arrow \(\langle r,u:c\to Gr\rangle\), i.e. every \(f:c\to Gd\) factors uniquely as \(Gf'\circ u\); then \[ \mathscr{D}(Fc,d)\cong\mathscr{C}(c,Gd)\quad\text{naturally in }c,d, \] and the free vector space \(X\xrightarrow{j}U(V_X)\) or the completion \(X\xrightarrow{\eta_X}U(\overline{X})\) of a metric space are characterised entirely by what they do to maps, never by what they are made of. The Yoneda lemma is the terminus of this logic: \(\mathrm{Nat}(\mathscr{C}(-,a),P)\cong P(a)\), via \(\eta\mapsto\eta_a(\mathrm{id}_a)\), so the embedding \(\mathrm{y}:\mathscr{C}\to[\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}]\) is fully faithful and \[ \mathscr{C}(-,a)\cong\mathscr{C}(-,b)\iff a\cong b. \] An object is what the morphisms into it say it is, together with their behaviour under precomposition; intrinsic predicates have no purchase, because there is nothing intrinsic for them to grasp. This is relational individuation in the strict sense of Assadian and Fraser, and its ontological mechanics are now visible: identity is not denied but relocated, from a membership tree to a representable presheaf [PDF, Ch. 2 §III].

The relocation has a price. Yoneda delivers \(a\cong b\), never \(a=b\); and an isomorphism, unlike an identity, is data. When \(\mathrm{Iso}(a,b)\) is non-empty it is a torsor under \(\mathrm{Aut}(a)\), and its elements are not interchangeable: \(\mathrm{Iso}(K^n,V)\) is a \(\mathrm{GL}_n(K)\)-torsor, one isomorphism per basis, and nothing in \(V\) singles one out. Universal properties tame this only partially—they pin the occupant down up to a unique isomorphism once the universal element is fixed—but the occupant itself still carries its automorphisms, and sameness between objects has become a set rather than a truth value. Meanwhile the ambient foundation has not gone away. So long as categories are built in set theory, \(\mathrm{Ob}(\mathscr{C})\) is a set (or class) and \(a=b\) is the material equality of §I, a proposition; there is a canonical map \((a=b)\to\mathrm{Iso}(a,b)\), sending reflexivity to \(\mathrm{id}_a\), and it is in general very far from a bijection. Two notions of sameness coexist, and the theory has no means of reconciling them. The symptoms are the ‘evil’ statements: ‘\(\mathrm{Ob}(\mathscr{C})\) has exactly \(n\) elements’, ‘\(\mathscr{C}\) is skeletal’, ‘\(Fa=b\)’ on the nose, all well-formed and none invariant. The same split recurs one level up: an equivalence \(F:\mathscr{C}\rightleftarrows\mathscr{D}:G\) with \(\eta:\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{C}}\cong GF\) and \(\varepsilon:FG\cong\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{D}}\) is the right notion of sameness for categories, while an isomorphism \(GF=\mathrm{id}\), \(FG=\mathrm{id}\) is strictly stronger and itself evil—a category and its skeleton are equivalent and, in general, not isomorphic. Invariance is thus a norm, the principle of equivalence, which every categorist obeys and no categorical theorem enforces. The moral vocabulary is revealing: a framework that must forbid what it can express has not yet internalised its own insight. Category theory as a language is a unifying grammar of considerable reach; as an answer to the problem of unity it is a partial yes, partial exactly at identity.

Category theory as a foundation narrows the gap without closing it. In an elementary topos logic is secreted by the structure rather than laid over it: \(\mathrm{Sub}(A)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(A,\Omega)\) naturally in \(A\) via the pullback of \(\mathrm{true}:1\to\Omega\), each \(\mathrm{Sub}(A)\) is a Heyting algebra, and the quantifiers are adjoints to pullback, \(\exists_f\dashv f^*\dashv\forall_f\). Add classifying toposes, \(\mathbf{Geom}(\mathcal{E},\mathbf{Set}[\mathbb{T}])\simeq\mathbb{T}\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{E})\), and Morita equivalences as Caramello’s bridges, and logical intelligibility springs from mathematical structure and travels between universes—here a foundation first meets Cavaillès halfway. Only halfway, because the constitutive gestures accumulate (a natural numbers object postulated; Booleanness by stipulation, as in ETCS, or by retreat to \(\mathrm{Sh}_{\neg\neg}(\mathcal{E})\); Cauchy and Dedekind reals coinciding only under further principles), because the 2-category \(\mathbf{Topos}\) that stitches toposes together is not an object of any topos, and above all because a 1-topos inherits the two defects just diagnosed: objects are still discrete, and the hom-sets are still sets [PDF, Ch. 3 §II.2].

Of these defects, the second is where the whole argument turns. In a 1-category, parallel \(f,g:a\to b\) are equal or not, and there is nowhere to record a homotopy between them; one may quotient such homotopies away, as in \(\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Top})\), but the quotient has products and not, in general, pullbacks or pushouts, and homotopy limits are not its limits—the information discarded was exactly what the constructions needed. Likewise, moduli problems whose objects carry non-trivial automorphisms live in stacks, not in sheaves of sets. The pattern is general: sameness of objects requires isomorphisms, so the core \(\mathscr{C}^{\simeq}\) is a groupoid; sameness of functors requires natural isomorphisms, so \(\mathbf{Cat}\) is a 2-category; sameness of categories requires equivalences, which live one level higher still. Each time one climbs, the identity of the level below turns into structure, and a 1-categorical foundation simply cuts the ladder. Climbing it in earnest leads to \((\infty,1)\)-categories, where \(\mathscr{C}(a,b)\) is replaced by a mapping space \(\mathrm{Map}(a,b)\) with coherences in every dimension. Yet even there, if the ambient foundation is material, object identity remains a proposition external to the structure. The canonical map \[ \mathrm{idtoiso}_{a,b}:(a=b)\longrightarrow(a\cong b) \] exists in any setting where identity can be eliminated—reflexivity goes to the identity—and the question no set-based foundation, however many dimensions it equips its categories with, can pose internally is whether this map is an equivalence. To pose it, \(a=b\) must itself be a type with structure, a space of identifications rather than a truth value. That is the threshold HoTT crosses, and univalence is the answer it gives [PDF, Ch. 2 §II.2].

III. Paths: Identity with Thickness

Axiom (Univalence). The canonical map \(\mathrm{idtoeqv}:(A=_{\mathcal{U}}B)\to(A\simeq B)\) is an equivalence; hence \[ (A=_{\mathcal{U}}B)\simeq(A\simeq B). \] For univalent categories, analogously, \(\mathrm{idtoiso}_{a,b}:(a=b)\to(a\cong b)\) is an equivalence for all objects \(a,b\).

¶ Univalence closes the gap left by Yoneda, not by adding a criterion of identity but by abolishing the distance between identity and equivalence. By transport, every family \(P:\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{U}\) yields \((A\simeq B)\to P(A)\to P(B)\), so that every property expressible in the theory is invariant under equivalence—and a property that is not invariant is not forbidden, as in categorical practice, but simply unwritable. The principle of equivalence ceases to be an ethics and becomes a grammar. Benacerraf’s problem is dissolved at the root, since membership is not structure-invariant and ‘\(1\in 3\)’ has no counterpart for an arbitrary type; identity, everywhere else a metatheoretic judgement standing above the system, becomes a type inside it; and through the Rezk completion, every precategory being weakly equivalent to a univalent one, relational determination is internalised independently of presentation. What emerges is a structural form of Leibniz’s identity of indiscernibles, with indiscernibility taken relative to the relations an object bears to all others, whose converse, the indiscernibility of identicals, holds by transport. I take this to be the most philosophically charged result of the whole comparison, because Leibniz returns here with his metaphysics turned inside out. For Leibniz, identity rests on an inner content—every predicate contained in the complete concept, inexhaustible save to God; in a univalent category, it rests on an outer profile—a place in a web of morphisms, which the theory itself can survey. The notio completa has migrated from the subject to the relational stratum, and the homotopical paradigm inherits Leibniz’s principle while abandoning his ground [PDF, Ch. 2 §II.2].

Yet the categories so obtained are one-dimensional fragments of the theory that contains them. Hom-types are sets, and so are iso-types; hence the type of objects of a univalent category is a 1-type, the loops \(a=a\) form a group, and above them there is nothing. The ambient theory is incomparably thicker: by Hofmann–Streicher, Lumsdaine and van den Berg–Garner, every type is a weak \(\infty\)-groupoid, stratified by \[ \mathrm{isContr}(A):=\textstyle\sum_{a:A}\prod_{x:A}(a=x),\qquad \text{is-}(n{+}1)\text{-type}(A):=\textstyle\prod_{x,y:A}\text{is-}n\text{-type}(x=_A y), \] and while the full theory lives in \((\infty,1)\)-toposes, the categories it defines internally are flat; to reach the rest, hom-sets must give way to mapping spaces, with coherence data in every dimension, so that relational individuation does not stop at arrows but passes into homotopy spaces. Is all this higher structure idle? Truncation is available at every level, and most mathematics lives comfortably among sets. Granted—my claim is accordingly modest in form: not that every object carries non-trivial identifications in all dimensions, but that a foundation for the whole of mathematics must be able to treat them rigorously where they occur. And they do occur outside homotopy theory, physics supplying the cleanest case I know. For an abelian 2-form gauge field \(B\), the curvature of a gerbe with connection, gauge transformations act as \(B\mapsto B+d\rho_1\); but \(\rho_1\) and \(\rho_1+d\rho_0\) induce the same transformation, since \(d^2=0\), and these transformations between transformations make the configuration space, with its full hierarchy of equivalences, a 2-groupoid rather than a set or a groupoid, just as a \(p\)-form field demands a \(p\)-groupoid. Physical redundancy already exhibits the unbounded hierarchy, one finite stage at a time [PDF, Ch. 2 §III].

On the semantic side, Shulman’s theorem gives every Grothendieck \((\infty,1)\)-topos strict univalent universes, so HoTT is interpreted in all of them; that it is their internal language is expected and, as far as I know, established only in partial form. Univalent syntax above, higher-sheaf semantics below: here the ontological temptation becomes nearly irresistible, for if an object is exhaustively determined by its equivalences in every dimension, what is left of it beneath the relational stratum? Nothing—no atom, no matter, no hypokeimenon. I accept the conclusion, but in a precise sense: what dissolves is not the object as a formal element but its claim to fundamental ontological autonomy, and I call the resulting position a categorially invariant structural ontology. Its resemblance to eliminative ontic structural realism is obvious, \[ \mathcal{S}\prec_{\mathrm{ont}}x_i,\qquad \mathrm{Id}(x_i)\equiv\mathrm{Pos}_{\mathcal{S}}(x_i),\qquad \neg\,\mathrm{Prim}(x_i), \] and so is its resemblance to gauge theory: if \(\mathcal{O}(A)=\mathcal{O}(A^g)\), physical content resides in what is invariant across representations, as arithmetical content does across Zermelo and von Neumann. But I refuse to let the analogy do work it cannot do. The passage from mathematical invariance to the ontological priority of structure is an interpretive thesis of structural realism, not a theorem of topos theory or of HoTT, and nothing in my argument about mathesis depends on it. What I hold firmly is weaker and more defensible: structure is not what objects possess, but that by virtue of which they can be determined as objects at all.

IV. Mathesis without Substance

¶ The history fixes what ‘unity’ may mean. The usual story begins with Descartes, but Rabouin has shown that it should begin with Aristotle, sworn enemy of everything the modern mathesis would become, who nonetheless could not avoid conceding it a foothold. Against Plato’s synoptic dialectician he insisted that a demonstration valid for numbers does not transfer to magnitudes; yet he admitted a universality internal to the discipline—the universal demonstration of the permutability of proportions (An. Post. I 5), once proved separately for numbers, lines, solids and times—while forbidding any superscience of pure relations, because a relation cannot be conceived without a subject (Metaph. N 1). Read against this ban, the trajectory from the cumulative hierarchy to univalence is its progressive lifting: a science of relations that no longer requires a subject beneath them. Descartes did not lift it. His mathesis of ordo et mensura, nulli speciali materiae addictas sounds like the emancipation of relation from genus, but on Rabouin’s reading every question is still referred to a common subject, figured extension, and its identification with the algebra of La Géométrie is a misreading Descartes himself refused; Aristotle’s demand for a subject is not abolished, only displaced onto what the imagination can display. Leibniz went further, and in a different direction. He granted the sign genuine operative autonomy—demonstration vi formae, cogitatio caeca, the chiliagon handled without picturing a thousand sides—while denying it semantic self-sufficiency, since meaning is deferred, held in potestate, never generated by the calculus; and that tension between operative autonomy and semantic heteronomy is, to my mind, the true Leibnizian legacy.

Then comes Kant, who breaks the classical mathesis at its hinge. If arithmetic and geometry are synthetic a priori, the scale of identities on which Leibniz built his infinite analysis loses its first rung, and the complete convergence of all laws into a single system ceases to be a property of the world, becoming instead a focus imaginarius, a regulative idea that guides inquiry without ever becoming its object. I accept this limit without reservation. Cavaillès supplies the last constraint, the one that bites hardest on foundations: mathematics develops by an internal necessity, through tensions its own concepts generate, not along a straight line towards a fixed horizon. My conception of mathesis is assembled from these four inheritances—Ancient internal unity, Leibnizian scope, Kantian regulative restriction, Cavaillès’s genetic criterion—and it is the last that decides between the foundations [PDF, Ch. 3 §I]. What none of these authors offered, however, was a definition of unity I could actually use. I searched for one in them, and further afield, in Bacon and van Roomen, and found aspiration without rigour; the fault is not theirs, since the mathematics they knew differs in kind from the mathematics I work in, where higher toposes and univalent universes have changed what ‘common ground’ can even mean. I have therefore constructed one.

Definition (Strong and Weak Mathematical Unity). A mathematical system \(\mathfrak{M}\) is unified relative to a foundation \(\mathbb{F}\) when its components and constitutive relations can be constructed and articulated from a common conceptual basis, by a unified set of formation rules that preserves their structural, operational and inferential organisation: \[ \mathrm{MU}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathfrak{M})\iff\exists E_{\mathbb{F}}\,\big[\,\mathrm{Gen}_{\mathbb{F}}(E_{\mathbb{F}},\mathfrak{M})\wedge\mathrm{Pres}_{\mathbb{F}}(E_{\mathbb{F}},\mathfrak{M})\,\big], \] where \(\mathrm{Gen}\) requires every \(c\in C(\mathfrak{M})\) to be buildable from the basis and \(\mathrm{Pres}\) requires every \(r\in\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{M})\) to be preserved. Weak unity restricts \(E\) to non-trivial proper parts, \(C_E\subsetneq C(\mathfrak{M})\), \(\mathcal{R}_E\subsetneq\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{M})\), preordered by \[ E_1\preceq E_2\iff C_{E_1}\subseteq C_{E_2}\wedge\mathcal{R}_{E_1}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{E_2}, \] so that unification becomes a gradient rather than a binary.

The definition states a necessary condition, not a discriminating one: \((\mathbb{N},+,0)\) is unified relative to HoTT, but it is unified relative to ZFC as well, and on representability with preservation ZFC is formidable. What separates the frameworks is not whether they can build a given structure but how they build the passage between structures—whether identity, equivalence and transport belong to the theory or hover above it. In ZFC, \(\mathrm{Pres}\) is discharged by exhibiting a homomorphism such as \(\iota:\mathbb{N}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\), \(n\mapsto[(n,0)]\), and verifying its properties case by case; in HoTT, once a structure is a type, preservation along an equivalence is transport, and comes with the structure rather than after it. That is why the burden of the argument falls on Cavaillès rather than on the formalism, and why the definition is paired with an assessment of foundations, \(\mathfrak{A}:\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{D}\to\{0,1,2\}^7\), over the scientific domains \(\mathcal{D}\) in which mathematical structures bear—coherence \(C\), expressive adequacy \(E\), structural fidelity \(S\), interfoundational interoperability \(I\), formal verifiability \(V\), epistemological transparency \(T\), unificatory capacity \(U\)—whose worked cases and comparative matrix are in [PDF, Ch. 3 §III]. On this basis the reinterpretation takes the following form.

Thesis (Mathesis Universalis Reinterpreted). Let \(\mathcal{F}=\{F_{\mathrm{ZFC}},F_{\mathrm{Cat}},F_{\mathrm{HoTT}}\}\) and let \(\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{F}\) be a configuration. Then \[ \mathrm{MU}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\iff\bigwedge_{F\in\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{Adm}(F)\;\wedge\;\bigwedge_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\Big[\exists F\in\mathcal{C}\,\big(E(F,d)=S(F,d)=U(F,d)=2\big)\wedge\Big(|\mathcal{C}|>1\Rightarrow\bigwedge_{F\in\mathcal{C}}I(F,d)=2\Big)\Big], \] and the central claim is \[ \mathrm{MU}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\;\Longrightarrow\;F_{\mathrm{HoTT}}\in\mathcal{C}, \] with each \(F\in\mathcal{C}\) admitting an interpretation \(\iota_F:F\to F_{\mathrm{HoTT}}\) that preserves the structural, operational and inferential organisation of its components in the sense of the definition above:

¶ The shape of the claim matters as much as its content. HoTT is placed at the centre of a configuration as the architecture through which the other systems are articulated and assessed, without being crowned their successor; ZFC keeps its sets and Cat its arrows, and what they lose is only the right to adjudicate identity from outside. If I am right, the old mathesis was not wrong to seek a centre—it was wrong to look for one made of substance. Four demands are held together in this centre, each answered by a different feature of the theory. Unity without reduction: integration into a coherent order with no single kind of entity beneath it. Leibniz recovered: under propositions-as-types a proposition \(P\) is established by exhibiting a term \(p:P\), so that characteristica and calculus ratiocinator—universal representation and systematic deduction—become one and the same calculus, and the old tension between the sign’s operative autonomy and its semantic heteronomy is resolved in favour of the former, at least within mathematics. Cavaillès honoured: the hierarchy of universes \(\mathcal{U}_0:\mathcal{U}_1:\mathcal{U}_2:\cdots\), higher inductive types and the homotopical reading of structure give an architecture that extends without closing. And Kant obeyed, which is where a reinterpreted mathesis could most easily relapse into the imperialism of the old one, and which therefore cannot be left to good intentions.

I do not believe formalisation extends without limit to every domain of inquiry, so the limit is written into the definition: for each domain the criteria of scientificity split as \(\mathcal{R}_d=\mathcal{R}_d^{\mathrm{form}}\sqcup\mathcal{R}_d^{\mathrm{emp}}\), and mathesis is confined to the projection \[ \pi_{\mathrm{form}}\,\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{R}_d}(K):=\big(c_1(K),c_2(K),c_3(K)\big) \] onto conceptual consistency, systematic coherence and justification, while controllability, intersubjectivity and corrigibility remain the property of the discipline. Physical cosmology shows that the cut is not cosmetic. Its formalism lives almost entirely in pseudo-Riemannian geometry, and the chain from a Lorentzian metric through \(G+\Lambda g=8\pi G_{\mathrm N}T\) and the Friedmann equation to linear perturbations is internal and deductive; but the background solution says nothing about the statistics of the perturbations, and the passage to \(P(k)\), to \(C_\ell\), and to an estimator \(\hat C_\ell\) from the single sky we have, bounded by cosmic variance \(\Delta C_\ell/C_\ell=\sqrt{2/(2\ell+1)}\), is statistical and external. Two fields could hardly be closer in formalism or further apart in inferential regime, and if that is so for cosmology and geometry, it is so a fortiori for mathematics and the social sciences, where no formal kinship even softens the difference. A mathesis that ignored this would be naïve rather than universal [PDF, Ch. 3 §IV].

What remains open must be said plainly, since a foundation that hides its seams is exactly what I have been arguing against. Size is handled by a hierarchy of universes; the standard semantics in Kan complexes is carried out inside ZFC with inaccessible cardinals, so that the autonomy of HoTT remains answerable to the very framework it decentres—an irony I do not think fatal, but will not pretend away; excluded middle and the coincidence of Cauchy and Dedekind reals are delicate constructive choices, not givens; only the \((-1)\)-truncated types behave as propositions in the classical sense; and judgemental equality \(\equiv\) stays stubbornly outside the theory, the last trace of an identity that is decided rather than constructed. HoTT is therefore neither the realisation of the early-modern project nor the closure of the foundational question, and I would distrust anyone who sold it as either. Hence the spectre. Mathesis haunts the foundations of mathematics because the demand for unity outlives every substance proposed to satisfy it—Aristotle’s genera, Descartes’ extension, Leibniz’s complete concepts, the cumulative hierarchy—each a body offered to the ghost, and each the wrong kind of body. The homotopical turn offers a different kind of house rather than another body: unity as a network of relations, invariant under equivalence and open to its own transformation, in which the demand can dwell without being laid to rest.
